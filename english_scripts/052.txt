SCENARIO 052
锁等待与长事务

B / Developer: Several account updates have been waiting for six minutes. Can we terminate the blocking session now and let those requests move forward?

A / Ops Engineer: The database administrator identified a long-running transaction at the head of the blocking chain. We need to assess its rollback impact before terminating it.

C / Database Administrator: It belongs to the reconciliation job and has modified about four hundred thousand rows. Termination could trigger a lengthy rollback while locks remain held.

B / Developer: So killing the session wouldn't necessarily release everything immediately. Do we know whether the job is still making progress or waiting on something else?

C / Database Administrator: Its processed row count is increasing, and it isn't waiting on another lock. However, the current transaction is much larger than the job's usual batches.

A / Ops Engineer: I'll contact the job owner to understand the batch change and expected remaining work. We shouldn't invent a completion time from one progress sample.

B / Developer: Can we pause new reconciliation work without interrupting the active transaction? That might keep the problem from repeating while we assess the options.

C / Database Administrator: Yes, the scheduler supports pausing future batches. The owner must confirm that control, and we should also limit automatic retries from blocked application requests.

A / Ops Engineer: I'll give the incident lead two options: allow monitored completion or authorize termination with a potentially long rollback. We'll include current business impact for each.

B / Developer: I'll check whether the blocked requests can expire safely and confirm that customers won't receive duplicate updates when they try again later.

C / Database Administrator: I'll collect progress and lock measurements for the next five minutes. If termination is chosen, I'll execute it under approval and monitor rollback explicitly.

A / Ops Engineer: Let's review those measurements with the job owner and incident lead at that checkpoint. Recovery requires completed account updates, not just a disappeared session.
