B / Engineering Manager: Does that mean ninety-five percent of customers are waiting three seconds, or are we talking about a smaller share of the traffic?

A / Ops Engineer: It describes requests, not individual customers. Roughly ninety-five percent completed within three seconds in that window; the remaining requests took longer.

C / Application Engineer: The change is concentrated in product search. Other endpoints look stable, but search traffic also increased, so the comparison needs a consistent request mix.

A / Ops Engineer: Let us compare the same endpoint and region across matching fifteen-minute windows, then separate cached searches from requests that reach the database.

A / Ops Engineer: There were forty thousand search requests in the affected window. I will check histogram coverage and sample counts before relying on the comparison.

C / Application Engineer: The slower traces show database calls taking most of the time. That narrows the investigation, but it does not establish why those calls slowed.

A / Ops Engineer: Please compare query plans and connection wait times. I will check regional resource pressure and whether the traffic change affected cache hit rates.

B / Engineering Manager: Bring the findings to our 13:30 UTC checkpoint. Include request counts and customer-facing response times so we can judge whether mitigation is needed.

A / Ops Engineer: Understood. We will report the regional tail latency alongside the average, and clearly separate measured behavior from our current explanations for it.
